are reduced, denominators positive, radical radicands squarefree and >1.

Coverage decisions and exclusions
Grade grounding: src/lib/middle/m1Geo2.ts (polygons), m1Geo3.ts (circles/sectors),
m1Solid.ts plus m1Book2.ts/m1Stat.ts (solids), m3Trig.ts (ratios and special-angle
length/area), m3AngleApply.ts and m3Tangent.ts (circle angle/chord/tangent).
This is engine-grounded placement, not a new official achievement-standard audit.

Parallel lines are explicitly given; the family computes a supplement across
two intersections. Basic e4 complementary/supplementary and existing triangle
interior sums are not duplicated. Exterior-angle forward/inverse remain here.
Same-arc equality is intentionally retained as requested concept practice;
copy-only corresponding/alternate/vertical-angle types are not inflated.
Polygon n=3..12; inverse regular-angle tasks use integer degree inputs; direct
regular angles may have rational answers (e.g. n=7). Inverse diagonals excludes
n=3 so it practices a positive diagonal count. Counting families use 개.
Following m1Geo2.ts:925 and root's review, the three inverse side-count families
are text-only. Root owns additional inverse-sum and polyhedron count families.
Circle radius inverses use perfect-square pi coefficients, avoiding middle1
radicals. Sector domains include reflex sectors 240° and 270°; drawings use
the correct major arc. Inverse angle/radius inputs show the measured arc/area,
not the unknown numeric radius/angle. There is no hidden answer text in SVG.
Solid volumes use explicit base area/height or radius/height. Prism surface
domains use realizable rectangular bases (base area and perimeter both given),
and explicitly right prisms. Pyramid surface uses a regular square pyramid
with face slant height s > half base edge. Cone surface uses l > r and labels
l as generator; h remains the perpendicular height. Sphere answers include
exact fractional pi volumes. Every 3-D instruction explicitly says schematic.
Direct solid inverses include prism/pyramid base area and perpendicular height
from volume, right-prism height/base area from surface, square-pyramid face
height from surface, cylinder/cone radius/height from volume, cylinder
radius/height from lateral area and height from surface, cone radius/generator
from lateral area and generator from surface, and sphere radius from area or
volume. Square/cube root answers are exact positive integers. Existing explicit
inverse examples: m1Solid.ts:1360 (face height), :1385 (cone generator), :1472
(pyramid height), :1506 (cone height); m1Stat.ts:432 recovers a sphere radius
from volume in its surface-area conversion. No new middle1 radical notation.
Chord length and distance are direct one-right-triangle circle applications;
generic Pythagoras, centers, similarity and middle2 quadrilaterals belong to
the other worker and are not duplicated. Trig ratios use actual right-triangle
coordinates and supplied legs/hypotenuse. Special length combines all six
given/target side pairs, including rationalized inverse ratios; area uses two
given sides with included 30°,45°,60°,90°. No high-school trig laws or domains.
Tangent-chord angles support a given central angle (half) or an inscribed angle
on the same intercepted arc (equal); source m3AngleApply.ts:1363-1418.
Equal tangent lengths from one exterior
point are retained as explicitly requested core relation practice; both drawn
radius/tangent pairs independently measure 90°.
Excluded: proofs, hidden auxiliary construction, tangent chains, composite
shaded/solid subtraction puzzles, arbitrary approximate angles/lengths,
surface area from insufficient base/slant data, general oblique solids,
non-elementary inverse roots, and reading lengths off an unlabelled sketch.
Cylinder/cone radius from total surface area plus height/generator is omitted:
the general form is quadratic, unlike the direct linear or perfect-root
inversions above. Power-of-point and hidden tangent constructions are excluded.
These require reasoning, approximation, unsupported grade content or more
givens than a direct arithmetic drill supplies.
