Coverage and grade decisions
- Acute-triangle circumcenter: central/vertex/radius-base angles and inverses,
  the three radius-base angles, equal radii, right-triangle hypotenuse/radius,
  and the radius from two given legs. Circumcenters are actual equidistant points.
- Obtuse-triangle circumcenter: small central angle 360-2A, inverse vertex
  angle and radius-triangle base angle A-90. A=100..150 in steps of 5; O is
  truly across BC from A, and the prompt explicitly distinguishes the minor
  central angle. Both rays and the correct minor arc are drawn. Arc radii adapt
  to nearby sides so a near-straight central arc does not cross BC.
- Incenter: half/whole angles, 90+A/2 and inverse, three half-angles,
  isosceles base half-angle, area/radius/perimeter, a component triangle area,
  and right-triangle inradius. Radius, area, perimeter and coordinates all agree;
  the finite metric examples are scaled 3-4-5 and 5-12-13 triangles.
- Centroid: all six direct median/long-part/short-part directions, 1/3 and 1/6
  areas, and the sum of the three vertex-to-centroid segments.
- Isosceles apex bisector/altitude/base properties (plain apex/base angle drills
  remain the elementary worker's scope). Midsegment/inverse, quarter/remainder
  area, medial perimeter/inverse, median areas, and explicit same-height area ratio.
- Similarity: corresponding length, perimeter, area and volume, enlarge/reduce,
  powers of the length ratio and inverse square/cube ratios. Volume diagrams are
  explicitly the corresponding triangular bases of two similar triangular prisms.
- Parallel section proportions in triangles, three parallel lines, trapezoid
  midline/inverse, diagonal midpoint separation, general parallel section; internal
  and external angle-bisector segment proportions (external ray is actually drawn).
- Parallelogram adjacent angles, diagonal bisection/inverse and quarter area;
  rectangle cross-diagonal halves; rhombus diagonal angle; isosceles-trapezoid angle.
- Pythagoras: missing hypotenuse/leg, rectangle diagonal/side, isosceles altitude,
  rhombus side, integer and radical domains, drawn altitude/projection/area,

Finite-domain and exact-answer conventions
- Domains are constructed explicitly, never random retries or advertised capacity.
  Pythagoras legs: integers 3..20, a<=b, b<=3a. Known leg/hypotenuse: a=3..20,
  c=5..25, positive other leg with aspect ratio between 1/3 and 3. Perfect-square
  and non-square domains are disjoint. Each title corresponds to a different
  requested geometric quantity, not a reworded duplicate task.
- Acute center-angle examples use A=40..80 in steps of 4. Obtuse examples have
  distinct type identities, actual outside centers and dedicated numerical gates.
- Length and centroid scalar domains are generally 2..20. Ratio pairs are reduced,
  1<=p<q<=5, p<=4, q<=3p. Exact domain sizes are listed below.
- Integer exact=[n]; rational exact=[reduced n, positive d]. Surd exact=[c,r,d]
  means c*sqrt(r)/d, with squarefree r>1, positive integers and gcd(c,d)=1.
  Perfect-square radicals collapse to integer/rational. There is no pi family here.
  Answer text and latex contain only the answer with its unit, never working.
- Oversized hostile exact components are rejected before root factorization.
